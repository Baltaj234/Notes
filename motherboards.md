# Motherboards

## What Is a Motherboard?
- A motherboard is the main circuit board inside a computer.
- It connects all the computer's components so they can communicate with each other.
- It distributes power and allows components to work together.
- Most components either connect directly to the motherboard or communicate through it.

## Main Components of a Motherboard

| Component | Purpose |
|---|---|
| CPU Socket | Holds the processor (CPU). |
| RAM Slots (DIMM) | Hold memory modules. |
| PCIe Slots | Connect graphics cards, network cards, and other expansion cards. |
| SATA Connectors | Connect HDDs and SATA SSDs. |
| M.2 Slots | Connect modern NVMe SSDs and some wireless cards. |
| Power Connectors | Supply electricity from the power supply (PSU). |
| BIOS/UEFI Chip | Stores firmware used to initialize hardware and start the computer. |
| CMOS Battery | Maintains the system clock and firmware settings when powered off. |
| USB Headers | Connect front-panel USB ports on a computer case. |
| Audio Connectors | Connect audio devices and front-panel audio ports. |

## Different Types of Motherboards

Motherboards come in different sizes, known as **form factors**. The form factor affects the motherboard's dimensions, expansion slots, and compatibility with computer cases.

### 1. ATX (Advanced Technology Extended)
- One of the most common motherboard sizes for desktop computers.
- Usually offers multiple RAM slots and expansion slots.
- Provides plenty of room for additional hardware.
- Commonly used in gaming PCs and workstation computers.

**Real-world examples:**
- ASUS ROG Strix B650E-F Gaming WiFi
- MSI MAG B650 Tomahawk WiFi
- Gigabyte Z790 AORUS Elite AX

### 2. Micro-ATX (mATX)
- Smaller than a standard ATX motherboard.
- Usually has fewer expansion slots.
- Generally more affordable.
- Good for budget gaming PCs and everyday desktop computers.

**Real-world examples:**
- ASUS PRIME B550M-A
- MSI PRO B760M-A WiFi
- Gigabyte B650M DS3H

### 3. Mini-ITX
- A very small motherboard form factor.
- Usually measures approximately 170 × 170 mm.
- Typically has two RAM slots and one main PCIe expansion slot.
- Used in compact desktop computers.
- Can be more expensive despite its smaller size.

**Real-world examples:**
- ASUS ROG Strix B650E-I Gaming WiFi
- Gigabyte B760I AORUS PRO
- ASRock B550 Phantom Gaming-ITX/ax

### 4. E-ATX (Extended ATX)
- Larger than standard ATX.
- Provides additional room for expansion and connectivity.
- Often used in high-end gaming PCs and professional workstations.
- Requires a compatible larger computer case.

**Real-world examples:**
- ASUS ROG Crosshair X870E Extreme
- MSI MEG X870E GODLIKE
- Gigabyte TRX50 AERO D

## Other Motherboard Categories

### Laptop Motherboards
- Designed specifically for laptops.
- Usually custom-built for a particular laptop model.
- Components such as the CPU and RAM may be soldered directly onto the board.
- Generally cannot be replaced with a standard ATX or Micro-ATX motherboard.

**Example:** ASUS Zenbook motherboard or Dell XPS motherboard.

### Server Motherboards
- Designed for servers that handle multiple users, applications, and services.
- Often support large amounts of RAM.
- May support multiple processors.
- Prioritize reliability, stability, and continuous operation.

**Real-world examples:**
- Supermicro X13 series server motherboards.
- ASUS Pro WS server/workstation boards.

### Embedded Motherboards
- Designed for specialized devices and machines.
- Often smaller and consume less power.
- Used in industrial equipment, networking devices, and embedded systems.

**Real-world examples:**
- Raspberry Pi 5 board.
- Industrial control computer boards.

## CPU Compatibility and Chipsets

- A motherboard must support the correct CPU socket for a processor.
- The chipset determines many motherboard features, such as connectivity, expansion, and supported functionality.
- Not every processor is compatible with every motherboard.

**Examples:**

| CPU Platform | Socket | Example Chipsets |
|---|---|---|
| AMD Ryzen 5000 | AM4 | A520, B550, X570 |
| AMD Ryzen 7000/9000 | AM5 | B650, X670, B850, X870 |
| Intel 12th–14th Gen | LGA 1700 | H610, B760, Z790 |
| Intel Core Ultra Desktop Series 2 | LGA 1851 | B860, Z890 |

## Motherboard Compatibility Considerations

When building or upgrading a computer, consider the following:

1. **CPU Socket:** Does the motherboard support your processor?
2. **RAM Compatibility:** Does it support DDR4 or DDR5 memory?
3. **Form Factor:** Will the motherboard fit inside your computer case?
4. **Expansion Slots:** Does it have enough PCIe slots for additional hardware?
5. **Storage:** Does it support the SATA and M.2 drives you want to install?
6. **Power:** Does your power supply have the required motherboard connectors?
7. **BIOS Compatibility:** Does the motherboard have firmware that supports your chosen CPU?

## Key Things to Remember
- The motherboard connects and allows communication between computer components.
- ATX, Micro-ATX, Mini-ITX, and E-ATX are motherboard form factors.
- The CPU socket determines which processors are compatible.
- The chipset determines many of the motherboard's capabilities.
- DDR4 and DDR5 are different RAM standards and are not interchangeable.
- A motherboard must physically fit inside the computer case.
- Always check hardware compatibility before purchasing components.
